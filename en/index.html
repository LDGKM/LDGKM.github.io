1 · The city

1,678 buildings and 533 trees, rebuilt

Central Mulhouse, each building in its construction year, from France's national map database (IGN). Trees aren't in it: I detected them myself in aerial photos.

What this showsBuilding a usable model by combining several public sources.

2 · Accidents

2,164 accidents, where they happened

This is the Haut-Rhin department. Each dot is an accident with at least one person injured, recorded by the police from 2019 to 2024.

What this showsCleaning and checking: 24 national files whose format changes every year, cleaned in SQL; my death counts for France match the official figures exactly.

Source: ONISR national accident file (BAAC), data.gouv.fr.

3 · Deaths

156 fatal accidents

In red, accidents in which at least one person died within 30 days.

Haut-Rhin has 36 road deaths per million inhabitants a year, against 46 in mainland France: the 11th safest of 96 departments.

What this showsComparing fairly: rates per inhabitant, not raw totals.

4 · The trap

Too few accidents in Mulhouse

Within 3 km of the centre: 115 accidents around Mulhouse, 157 around Colmar, a smaller town. Yet Mulhouse has more fatal accidents (14 against 12).

Minor accidents are very likely under-recorded there: in this area, only deaths are a reliable indicator.

What this showsSpotting a bias: checking that the data measure what we think before drawing a conclusion.

5 · Operating rooms

Planning on history: 3 rooms out of 4 run late

One simulated day built from real operations (open VitalDB dataset): 4 rooms of 8 hours. Transparent blocks are the plan, made with each procedure's usual duration; solid blocks are what really happened. In red, overtime.

That day: 111 minutes of overtime per room on average. Over 200 simulated days: 113.

6 · Uncertainty

With learned uncertainty: 18 minutes

The same day, each operation is planned with the duration it has an 80% chance of not exceeding, learned by a model (conformalized quantile regression), then placed by an optimisation solver.

That day: 18 minutes of overtime per room, scheduling 10 operations instead of 12. Over 200 days: 19 minutes, with 88.7% of room time used.

What this showsQuantifying uncertainty and using it to optimise a decision: this is my research project.
